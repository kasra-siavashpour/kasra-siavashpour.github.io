01 · Multi-agent systems

Fair routing when everyone shares the road

FAIR · IPM & Sharif

When every vehicle picks its own route, some trips pay for everyone else's speed. FAIR routes agents so that no one envies another's share of the delay, and the guarantee holds at every step of a trip. I designed and built the evaluation; when it contradicted two of our lemmas on real networks, we revised them.

Evaluated on 7 city road networks with up to 132K trips each.

Cooperation without a coordinator

Loop of the Rings · IPM & Sharif

The Loop of the Rings is a fully decentralized cooperative system built from randomized rings of participants. It can serve as a shared workspace or a platform for services, from freelancing to IoT management. FAIR: A Novel Envy-Free Multi-Agent Model for Traffic Systems
Arash Vaezi, Sepideh Niknejad, Kasra Siavashpour, Arshia Dadras
In preparationfor the International Conference on Autonomous Agents and Multiagent Systems (AAMAS) 2027 · abstract submitted
Abstract

We introduce FAIR (Fairness-Aware Incremental Routing), a dynamic multi-agent traffic system that enforces fairness in routing decisions while continuously adapting to real-time congestion. Agents move through a shared directed road network and repeatedly recompute shortest paths at intersections using density-aware edge weights derived from a non-linear car-following model. The system evolves through a sequence of discrete decision states, each encoding agent positions, local densities, and traversal costs, enabling agents to make informed, myopic routing decisions in a fully dynamic environment.

The initial fairness notion is optimal envy-freeness: the ratio of any two agents' expected remaining travel costs at departure must match the ratio of their realized costs upon arrival. Unlike classical equilibrium-based notions, the paper establishes a trajectory-level fairness guarantee: under mild structural assumptions, FAIR preserves optimal envy-freeness at every decision step along each agent's trajectory, independent of future arrivals, congestion evolution, or convergence to equilibrium. It also introduces proportional envy-freeness, a relaxed criterion allowing bounded deviations while ensuring comparable agents experience travel costs within a constant factor of each other.

Beyond fairness, we show that FAIR induces strong structural properties throughout system evolution. Specifically its: (1) routing decisions select edges that incur the smallest incremental delay rather than the currently fastest path (congestion-efficiency); (2) local congestion is balanced at every intersection, preventing any outgoing edge from becoming disproportionately loaded; and (3) the global traffic state evolves gradually and predictably, avoiding oscillations, sudden congestion spikes, or cascading effects (smoothness).

These properties also persist, up to constant factors, under proportional envy-freeness, demonstrating robustness to imperfect fairness.

Experiments on seven metropolitan road networks show that FAIR reduces the observed envy spread by factors of 9.1–22.9 relative to a dynamically recomputed free-flow shortest-path baseline, while also reducing travel time and delay and increasing throughput in all evaluated networks.

A Comprehensive Survey of Self-Organizing Networks in Next-Generation Cellular Systems
Ardalan Siavashpour, Kasra Siavashpour, Masoumeh Azadegan, Bardia Safaei, Hossein Peyvandi, Muhammad Imran
In preparationfor the IEEE Open Journal of the Communications Society (OJ-COMS)
Abstract

The increasing complexity of modern cellular networks, driven by ultra-dense deployments, heterogeneous architectures, and diverse service requirements, has rendered traditional manual network management approaches impractical. Self-Organizing Networks (SON) have emerged as a key enabler of autonomous operation by introducing self-configuration, self-optimization, and self-healing capabilities into radio access and backhaul infrastructures. Existing SON surveys, however, remain partial and 5G-centric. Broad treatments date from the early 5G period, while recent reviews examine a single function in isolation. Self-protection and self-learning are treated as prospective extensions rather than integral capabilities, and no survey analyzes the full SON function set in the context of 6G architectures. Motivated by these gaps, this paper presents a comprehensive survey of SON research, systematically reviewing architectural principles, closed-loop control mechanisms, performance objectives, control parameters, and algorithmic techniques across core SON functions. Beyond standardized 3GPP functionalities, emerging paradigms such as self-learning and self-protection are analyzed to capture the evolution of SON toward intelligent and predictive network management. The survey synthesizes optimization-based, heuristic, and learning-driven approaches, highlighting their trade-offs in scalability, adaptability, and operational robustness. Quantitative trends derived from 114 references illustrate the shifting research focus toward self-optimization and resilience in the 5G and beyond era. Finally, open challenges and future research directions are discussed, including conflict coordination among SON functions, explainable and federated learning, integration with non-terrestrial networks, and the role of SON in 6G-enabled architectures.

The Loop of the Rings: A Fully Decentralized Reliable Cooperative System
Arash Vaezi, Amir Daneshgar, Arshia Dadras, Kasra Siavashpour
In preparationfor the IEEE Symposium on Foundations of Computer Science (FOCS) 2027Preliminary version on arXiv
Abstract

We introduce LoR, a reliable, scalable, fully decentralized, and distributed cooperative system, where LoR stands for “Loop of the Rings”. Distinct from conventional transaction-oriented systems, LoR handles cooperation using its ring-based randomized structure, making it suitable as both a cooperative workspace and a versatile platform for service provisioning, accommodating various roles such as freelancers, IoT management systems, and even 5G-related services. In LoR users access a reliable environment, enabling them to either share in or offer a predetermined set of services initially determined by an admin. LoR may also be implemented as a middleware that operates independent of the underlying infrastructure. Assuming a proportion of at least 60 percent of honest participants in an instance of LoR, the system provides reliability, scalability and satisfaction i.e. users may make occasional mistakes or face temporary issues such as network delays, yet those who act correctly will ultimately be satisfied by receiving their services or payments.

In addition to introducing LoR's structure along with its operating rules in detail as our main contribution, we also provide mathematical analysis for the worst-case scenarios as well as a typical minimal real-case simulation supporting our theoretical results on LoR's reliability, scalability and user satisfaction.
